**Purpose:** Coalesce project ideas and form two groups for the two-week project.

## Key decisions

- Two project themes were established:
  - **Online/Continual Learning**
  - **Delayed Reward/Credit Assignment**
- Each group will implement one or two novel learning algorithms—such as Predictive Coding, BTSP, or SNN-based rules—and compare them with baselines such as Backpropagation and Hebbian learning.
- The primary evaluation will measure robustness in each group's chosen harder scenario: online learning or delayed reward.
- A secondary goal is to identify distinctive performance signatures, including learning speed, accuracy, and signal-to-noise ratio.
- The shared scientific motivation is to explore biologically plausible alternatives to Backpropagation that address the weight transport problem.

## Candidate approaches

- **Predictive Coding:** Local, error-driven updates as a biologically plausible response to the weight transport problem.
- **Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs):** Membrane-voltage and spike-time models, potentially paired with plasticity rules such as STDP. MNIST remains suitable because the project focuses on the learning rule rather than the data source.
- **BTSP:** A non-Hebbian, three-factor plasticity rule involving input, output, and a neuromodulatory signal, intended to associate events separated by seconds.
- **Eligibility traces:** A mechanism for connecting delayed rewards to earlier actions in credit-assignment tasks.

## Research directions

- **Learning well:** Test whether existing algorithms remain effective under harder conditions such as streaming data, noise, and non-stationarity, while limiting catastrophic forgetting.
- **Inferring rules:** Analyze the unique behavioral signatures of novel algorithms so they can be distinguished experimentally.
