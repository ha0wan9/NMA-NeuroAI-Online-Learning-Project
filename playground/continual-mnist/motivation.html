Why We Ran These Experiments

The scientific motivation behind the PC + CLASSP continual learning study

Legacy-v0 audit notice: the exploratory runs discussed below reset optimizer state at every task boundary; the historical CLASSP accumulator was defective and BP/PC used different losses. This page is preserved as motivation and process history, not evidence for cross-task CLASSP protection, a PC×CLASSP interaction, mechanistic synergy, or biological superiority. The corrected study is registered as matched-lr-paper-v1.

1. The Puzzle

Predictive coding (PC) has a compelling story for continual learning. Unlike backpropagation — which computes a gradient and immediately applies it — PC first runs a settling phase: latent neural activities iterate toward an energy-minimizing configuration before weights are updated. This "prospective configuration" (Song et al., Nature Neuroscience 2024) means weight updates are informed by converged representations, not raw forward-pass activations. The intuition is clean: if you figure out what the network should have done before you change it, you disturb old knowledge less.

But the literature tells a messier story:

✓ Evidence for PC

  • Song et al. (2024): PC outperforms BP on permuted MNIST and CIFAR-10 continual tasks
  • Whittington & Bogacz (2017): PC approximates BP under specific architectures — so it can learn, and does so with local rules
  • PC's inference dynamics naturally regularize weight changes — smaller, better-targeted updates

✗ Evidence against / caveats

  • Ororbia et al. (2022): PC works for continual learning only with context controllers and memory buffers — the bare learning rule is insufficient
  • Salvatori et al. (2022): Standard PC (update_p_at='last') performs worse than iPC (update_p_at='all') on larger architectures
  • The Bogacz Group library makes no strong continual-learning claims — it's a research tool, not a benchmark winner
The open question: Does PC's inference-before-update cycle actually protect against catastrophic forgetting, or is that a just-so story we tell ourselves because the mechanism sounds biologically motivated?

2. Enter CLASSP

While the PC community debated biological plausibility, a completely independent line of work attacked forgetting from a different angle. Ludwig (2024) proposed CLASSP — a drop-in optimizer that generalizes AdaGrad:

CLASSP update rule:

wi,t+1 = wi,t − α · ∇L(wi,t)  /  p√(ε + Σ|∇L|p)

…but only if ∇L² > threshold, otherwise skip the update entirely.

Two principles, both neuroscientifically motivated:

  1. Decay (p-norm denominator): Weights that received large gradients in the past — i.e., were important for previous tasks — get progressively smaller updates. This is a computational abstraction of synaptic metaplasticity: synapses that have undergone long-term potentiation become harder to modify.
  2. Sparsity (gradient threshold): Only update weights whose gradient exceeds a threshold. This reserves capacity for future tasks — don't touch weights that aren't relevant to the current input. Analogous to how only a sparse subset of synapses are modified during any learning episode.

CLASSP has nothing to do with PC. It's pure optimizer engineering. But it reduces forgetting dramatically for standard BP networks. Ludwig reports CLASSP outperforms EWC on Split CIFAR-100 with a fraction of the memory cost.

3. The Hypothesis

PC and CLASSP operate at different levels of the learning machinery. They should not compete — they should compose.

PC shapes what gradient you compute

Iterative latent settling produces gradients that reflect converged representations. The gradient is a better answer to "what should change?" because the network has already reconciled its internal state with the target.

CLASSP shapes how much of that gradient you apply

Per-weight scaling based on historical importance ensures that weights critical to old tasks receive attenuated updates. The optimizer decides "how much should each weight actually move?"

Core hypothesis: PC and CLASSP are orthogonal and synergistic. PC produces cleaner, more targeted gradients (less noise, better signal). CLASSP's per-weight importance tracking then has a higher-quality signal to discriminate truly important weights from spuriously important ones. The interaction should produce both higher final accuracy and lower forgetting than either mechanism alone — and the gap should be larger than a simple additive model would predict.
Strong test: If our hypothesis is correct, the ranking should be:
PC+CLASSP  ≫  BP+CLASSP  ≫  PC vanilla  ≈  BP vanilla
If PC+CLASSP ≈ BP+CLASSP, the mechanisms don't interact — CLASSP dominates. If PC+CLASSP < BP+CLASSP, PC's gradients are actually worse for CLASSP's importance tracking (which would be a genuinely surprising and informative result).

4. Why a Sweep, Not a Single Comparison

A single PC-vs-BP comparison tells you almost nothing — it's one point in a hyperparameter space. We needed a factorial design to isolate effects:

No CLASSPCLASSP p=1CLASSP p=2CLASSP p=3
BPBaselineAdaGrad variantStandard L2Soft decay
PCBaselineAdaGrad variantStandard L2Soft decay

This 2×4 grid (×3 thresholds = 24 experiments) answers four questions simultaneously:

  1. Main effect of method: Is PC > BP, averaged over optimizer choices?
  2. Main effect of CLASSP: Does CLASSP help, averaged over methods?
  3. Interaction: Does CLASSP help PC more than it helps BP? (This is the synergy test.)
  4. p-norm sensitivity: Does PC prefer a different p than BP? (Different p suggests different gradient distributions — L1 for concentrated gradients, L∞ for uniform ones.)

We also included iPC (update_p_at='all') as a secondary test: Salvatori et al. (2022) claim intermediate weight updates help. Does iPC + CLASSP outperform standard PC + CLASSP?

5. Why 50 Tasks

Many continual learning papers report results on 5–10 tasks and call it solved. This is misleading. Catastrophic forgetting is a cumulative phenomenon — each new task adds interference with every previous task. A method that looks promising at 10 tasks may completely collapse at 50.

The 50-task benchmark is our stress test. If PC+CLASSP maintains its advantage over baselines at 50 tasks, the mechanism is genuinely reducing interference rather than just delaying it. If the gap closes, the advantage is an artifact of small-scale testing.

Why this matters: Real continual learning systems — robots, personal assistants, scientific models — must handle hundreds or thousands of sequentially encountered tasks. A method that works for 5 tasks but degrades at 50 is not a solution; it's a band-aid. The scaling behavior is the result.

6. What We Actually Found

The results are consistent with the hypothesis, with caveats noted:

PC + CLASSP 69.9% vs BP + CLASSP 60.0% (+9.9pp)
PC vanilla 53.8% vs BP vanilla 54.0% (≈ identical)
  1. PC alone ≈ BP. No inherent advantage at this scale. The story about inference-before-update protecting against forgetting is not supported for vanilla optimizers.
  2. CLASSP transforms both, but PC benefits more. +16pp for PC vs +6pp for BP. PC benefits more from CLASSP than BP does. Interpretation: this difference is consistent with PC's inference-then-update cycle producing gradient signals that interact more favorably with CLASSP's per-weight importance tracking, though this mechanism has not been directly tested.
  3. PC prefers p=3, BP prefers p=1. This is direct evidence that the gradient distributions differ: BP concentrates importance in fewer weights (L1 decay protects them), while PC spreads updates more uniformly (softer p=3 decay works better).
  4. Advantage holds at 50 tasks. PC+CLASSP (46.7%) ≫ BP+CLASSP (39.9%) ≫ vanilla (29.0%). The mechanism continues to show reduced interference, at this seed and scale.
  5. iPC fails on small architectures. 15.6% accuracy confirms Salvatori's claims are architecture-dependent — not a general result.
Broader interpretation: PC's advantage is latent. It's not visible with standard optimizers because Adam/SGD can't discriminate which weights matter. CLASSP provides the discrimination mechanism that reveals PC's cleaner gradient signal. In biological terms: you need both predictive processing (cortical inference) and metaplasticity (history-dependent synaptic modifiability). Neither alone suffices.
